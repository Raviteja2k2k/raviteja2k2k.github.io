A 15-agent WhatsApp and Telegram platform for a real-estate brokerage

Enquiries are captured, qualified, routed, matched to properties and followed up by AI agents. The owner approves anything sensitive, gets a daily digest on Telegram and sees every lead in one dashboard. I designed, built and operate the whole system.

The problem

Enquiries arrived faster than people could answer them

Buyers write to the brokerage on WhatsApp and Telegram at all hours, often in Telugu written in English letters. Some enquiries went unanswered and some follow-ups slipped.

The brief: recover missed enquiries, keep a human in charge of anything sensitive, and run AI at a cost a small business can carry.

How it works

One message, five stages

  1. STAGE 1

    Capture

    WhatsApp Business API webhook with a signature check that fails closed and de-duplication; Telegram through a bot bridge.

  2. STAGE 2

    Record

    One writer service saves every write to SQLite, the source of truth. A Google Sheet is the owner's mirror, never the database.

  3. STAGE 3

    Qualify

    Rules first, then a small tool-less, memory-less extraction model turns each message into structured lead details.

  4. STAGE 4

    Act

    Routing, semantic property matching on local embeddings, six lead-risk scans and follow-ups on a written cadence.

  5. STAGE 5

    Report

    A deterministic daily digest on Telegram and an owner dashboard behind login.

Safety and governance

A human stays in charge

  • Engagement rules live in one versioned policy file: answer first, ask at most one or two questions, never state prices, legal status or RERA claims. Behaviour changes by editing the policy, not the code.
  • Follow-up cadence by lead temperature, quiet hours 21:00–09:00, and leads cool automatically after four ignored messages.
  • 284 WhatsApp template follow-ups in four weeks, each approved by the owner in the dashboard. A cold-lead reactivation agent was built and deliberately kept off.
  • Human takeover: when staff reply from the business number, the agents step back for 24 hours.
  • Owner notes on Telegram apply only when unambiguous; sensitive changes ask first, and every write returns an undo handle.
  • Every dashboard route needs login, with rate limits and an audit log. No customer data is pulled to my machines; backups stay on client-owned storage.
Reliability

Built to recover what would otherwise be missed

  • Single writer with a bounded lock, a journal, and a spool replayed every minute when the writer is busy; hourly snapshots and an AES-256 encrypted daily backup on client-owned storage. A restore drill passed.
  • A watchdog every 5 minutes with automatic rollback for the webhook sidecar, and a writer guardian that checks integrity, size and locks.
  • Outage recovery re-reads WhatsApp history in idle windows, and importers are idempotent by message id. A gap check once found about 20% of inbound messages missing; a gap importer was built to close it.
Lessons from 129 closed bugs

What I carry into every build

Never route single-purpose model calls through a full agent.

Extraction went from about 38 k to 4 k tokens a message.

Deterministic checks first, a model only on change.

Most scheduled checks need no model at all.

Signature checks must fail closed.

A webhook that guesses can be forged.

A spreadsheet is a view, not a database.

One writer, provenance and a change log.
